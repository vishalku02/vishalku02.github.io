Allora — so why do we salt the water?

It's not for time. Let's settle it.

what you said at the start:
salt makes water boil infinitesimally hotter
A normal pasta pot — ~10 g/L of salt — bumps the boil by about +0.09 °C.
That trims roughly 3 seconds off an 8-minute cook. A blink.

So the myth vs. the kitchen

the myth

"Aggiungi il sale — l'acqua bolle più veloce." Add salt so the water boils faster, and pasta cooks faster.

what's actually happening

Salt does raise the boiling point, but by a sliver — 0.5 g of salt per liter ≈ 0.005 °C. The cook-time savings are buried in noise.

Where the myth comes from: people do see salted water "stop bubbling" right after they dump salt in. That isn't the boiling point dropping — it's the nucleation sites where bubbles formed getting briefly disrupted as crystals dissolve. The temperature is unchanged.

Real reasons to salt the water

  1. Flavor. Pasta is starch — it's bland. The salt that diffuses in during boiling seasons the noodle itself in a way you can't replicate by salting at the table. This is the whole reason.
  2. Texture, a little. Salt makes starch granules swell slightly less, which can keep the pasta firmer on the surface. The effect is real but small at normal amounts.
  3. Sauce adhesion. Lightly salted pasta water becomes the seasoning for your sauce when you finish the pasta in the pan — a tablespoon of pasta water emulsifies and seasons in one move.

The Italian rule of thumb — "salty like the sea" — comes out to about 7–10 g/L, not the literal 35 g/L of seawater. Use that, and stop chasing the timer.
