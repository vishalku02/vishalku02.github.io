A normal pasta pot of salt buys you about 3 seconds off an 8-minute cook. For real-life perspective: humans can't taste a temperature difference under ~2 °C, and you'd need ~58 g/L (six times saltier than the sea) just to lift the boil by 1 °C.

The math, briefly

Salt raises water's boiling point by an amount called boiling-point elevation:

ΔT  =  Kb  ·  i  ·  m
ΔT  =  0.512  ·  2  ·  (grams / 58.44 / liters)   °C

For pasta water with 10 g of salt per liter, that's a boiling-point bump of about +0.09 °C. Reaction rates roughly follow Arrhenius — a rule of thumb is ~7% faster per °C — so the cook time shaves off roughly (1 − 1/(1 + 0.07·ΔT)) of the original. Tiny in. Tiny out.
